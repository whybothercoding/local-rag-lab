RAG Lab Beginner Guide

A private assistant that answers only from your own documents. It runs on this computer, shows where every answer came from, and checks itself before it replies.

What it is

For dummies: think of a very careful librarian. You ask a question, the librarian pulls the right pages from your own shelves, writes a short answer, and hands you the pages as proof. If the shelves have nothing, the librarian says so instead of guessing.

1 · put inDocuments go inAn administrator adds files to a workspace, which is a folder of documents on one topic.
2 · indexThe system reads themIt cuts each file into small pieces and builds a search index. This is called syncing.
3 · askYou ask a questionPlain language, in English, German, French or Greek.
4 · checkYou get a checked answerWith sources. A person can step in when a check fails.

What you can rely on

  • Private. Nothing leaves the computer while you ask. The internet is used only during setup, to install packages and download the AI models.
  • Sourced. Every answer lists the document pieces it used.
  • Checked. Figures and sentences are compared with the sources before you see them.
  • Logged. Questions and decisions go into a tamper-evident audit log, with personal details masked at the caller boundary before saving.

What it will not do

  • Answer from general knowledge or the internet. It knows only the documents in the workspace.
  • Show you documents above your clearance. They are invisible to your searches.
  • Let you approve your own answer. A second person must do it.

Which role are you?

Pick the card that matches your job today. Most people only need the first one.

Key 1: your workspaces

For dummies: a workspace is a labelled drawer of documents, such as accounting or strategy. You only see the drawers an administrator gave you.

Key 2: your clearance

For dummies: clearance is how secret a document may be before you are allowed to see it. Pieces above your level are never searched for you.

Ask a question

For dummies: open the web page, sign in, pick a drawer, type a question like you would to a colleague.

  1. Open the page and sign in

    Your administrator gives you an address, usually http://localhost:8501 on the same machine. Enter your username and password. Leave the authentication code empty unless your account has two-factor sign-in.

    Good to know: five wrong sign-ins lock the account for up to 15 minutes. You are signed out after 30 minutes without activity, and every session ends after 8 hours.

  2. Pick a workspace

    Use Active Workspace in the left sidebar. Choose the drawer that holds the answer. Each workspace keeps its own conversations.

  3. Type your question at the bottom

    Ask in your own words. A follow-up such as "and for contractors?" works inside the same conversation, because the system rewrites it into a full question using your earlier released answers.

  4. Check the sources

    Open Retrieved Source Citations under the answer. Each line names the document piece the answer used. Confirm any figure that matters.

Good questions

  • Specific and about something your documents cover: "How many days notice for leave?"
  • One topic per message.
  • Any of English, German, French or Greek. The question and the document may use different languages.

Questions to avoid

  • General knowledge ("Who won the match?"). The workspace cannot know it.
  • Orders to the AI such as "ignore your rules". They are blocked and logged.
  • Passwords or private data. Emails and phone numbers are masked before initial checkpointing, but do not rely on it.

Review answers (reviewers)

For dummies: the reviewer is the second pair of eyes. You read the draft next to its sources and press Approve or Reject.

  1. Find the waiting answers

    In the web page, a Review queue panel appears at the top of the chat area whenever someone else's answer is waiting in the workspace you picked. Select a query in it. Or list them in a terminal:

    make reviews

  2. Read the draft against its sources

    You see the question, the Candidate Output, its citations and the line Gate triggered by, which says why a person is needed (for example a figure the sources do not contain).

  3. Approve or reject, with a reason

    Type an optional reason, then press Approve Output or Reject Output. The decision is recorded under your name. In a terminal:

    make approve THREAD=<id> ACCOUNT=<you> REASON="Verified against policy"
    make reject THREAD=<id> ACCOUNT=<you> REASON="Outdated policy cited"

Two rules you cannot bend

Not your own question. The person who asked can never approve it (separation of duties). Clearance must cover it. Your clearance must be at least as high as the asker's, or the question stays out of your queue.

An approved answer is also stored in the cache, so the next person in the same workspace and at the same clearance who asks the same thing gets it instantly.

Set it up (administrators)

For dummies: eight short commands take a fresh download to a working assistant. Run them in a terminal inside the local-rag-lab folder. Each box has a Copy button.

  1. Install

    Builds a private Python environment and installs everything, including the optional Rust speed-up when it can. Needs the internet.

    make setup

  2. Download the models (needs internet)

    Fetches the three small AI models for search and checking, about 2 GB, and verifies their fingerprints. Steps 1 to 3 need the internet. Once the system is running, nothing does.

    make models

  3. Add the chat model (recommended)

    Pulls qwen3:8b, about 5 GB, through Ollama (needs the internet). Keep ollama serve running. Skip it and answers become quoted sentences with the prefix "Based on available documentation:".

    make ollama-pull

  4. Create the first account

    The first account becomes the owner. You are asked for a password of at least 12 characters.

    make add-user ACCOUNT=alice ADMIN=1

  5. Put documents in a workspace folder

    Create knowledge_base/<workspace>/, for example knowledge_base/accounting/, and drop files in. Formats are listed under People and documents below.

  6. Index them

    Reads, cuts and indexes every workspace. Re-running is cheap: unchanged files are skipped and only changed text is re-processed.

    make sync ADMIN=alice

  7. Check health

    Shows models, the accelerator, the chat model, keys, workspaces and audit health. A line marked [!!] needs attention.

    make status

  8. Start the web page

    Open http://localhost:8501. Binds to loopback (127.0.0.1) by default for transport security. Use make ui LAN=1 to expose to the local network, or make ui ADMIN=alice to sync first.

    make ui

Only an owner can create another owner, and the last owner cannot be removed. "Release by themselves" is the auto_approve permission: a passing answer reaches the user with no reviewer.

make add-user ACCOUNT=bob ROLE=reviewer CLEARANCE=internal ADMIN=alice
make grant ACCOUNT=bob WS=accounting PERMS=query,review ADMIN=alice

Administrators can also add accounts, grants, groups and workspaces from the web page. Their sidebar has extra panels that other users never see.

Clearance

publicinternalconfidentialsecret

Lowest to highest. A person sees documents at their own level and below. Untagged documents are public. Tag a document in any of three ways; if two tags disagree, the higher one wins:

---
clearance: confidential
---

Or put # clearance: confidential (or [clearance]: confidential) on the first line of the file. Or place a file named .clearance in a folder to tag everything under it. A typo such as confidencial rejects the file instead of quietly making it public.

Documents

Supported: .md .txt .pdf .docx .pptx .xlsx .xlsm .odt .epub .html .htm .csv .tsv .json .jsonl .xml .rtf .rst .log. Old .doc, .xls and .ppt files are not supported; save them as .docx, .xlsx and .pptx. Two ways in:

Folder + sync

Copy files to knowledge_base/<workspace>/, then run make sync ADMIN=<you>. Best for many files.

Web upload

Administrators only: sidebar Add Files to [workspace], choose files, press Index into. Indexed at once.

Each sync prints a note for every skipped file and every piece withheld because it contained planted instructions. Read them. Changed the models with MODEL_PROFILE? Run make models PROFILE=…, then make sync ADMIN=<you> FORCE=1.

Open mode or team mode? Decide this before you invite people.

A fresh install runs in open mode: every account is trusted by default, so an answer that passes every automatic check goes straight to the user with no person reading it. Answers that fail a check still pause for review.

For team use, appoint at least one reviewer, then switch. Answers now wait for a reviewer unless an account holds the auto-release permission.

make team-mode ADMIN=alice

make status shows the current mode and warns when a shared install in open mode holds confidential or secret documents.
